## 1. Abstract & Problem Statement
Traditional language learning methods and platforms rely heavily on text translation, visual reading, and prescriptive grammar rules. While effective for literacy, these methods ignore the core cognitive bottleneck in adult Second Language Acquisition (SLA): L1 phonetic filtering and the loss of real-time acoustic phoneme parsing in continuous speech.

Ground Level Languages is an acoustics-first acquisition methodology designed explicitly for adult cognition. By combining zero-literacy audio-visual coupling, descriptive acoustic analytics ($F_0$ pitch tracking and duration alignment on the backend), and concrete sensory narratives, the system enables adult learners to parse spoken language in real time before introducing written scripts.

The system prioritizes listening comprehension over speaking at Level 0. Because accurate sound perception directly drives both real-time comprehension and authentic vocal production, auditory discrimination is evaluated through binary acoustic choices (selecting between two options) rather than requiring vocal output or prescriptive tone labels.

---

## 2. Theoretical Framework & Pedagogy
1. **L1 Phonetic Interference & Sound Discrimination:** Prioritizes raw acoustic discrimination before text reading to bypass native sound filtering (Werker & Kuhl).
2. **Concrete Sensory Anchoring:** Eliminates native language (L1) translation by using concrete, animal-perspective graded reading narratives restricted to physical reality.
3. **Non-Prescriptive Ear Training:** Employs raw pitch isolation (humming) and descriptive sound maps without prescriptive tone marks or directional labels to accelerate ear training faster than passive immersion.

---

## 3. Core Technical Architecture
* **Acoustic Alignment Pipeline:** Integration of open-source alignment and pitch tracking tools (Allosaurus, MFA, CREPE) for descriptive phonetic analysis.
* **Zero-Text Learning Engine:** Interface presenting audio-first exposure, visual-only lookups, primers, and low-pressure review modules without graphemes or points/streaks.

---

## 4. Empirical Scope & Target Language Selection
To validate the universality of the Ground Level framework, the methodology will be evaluated across three distinct language tiers:
1. **High-Resource Languages (e.g., Mandarin Chinese & English):** Used to baseline acoustic pitch contour tracking ($F_0$) and evaluate adult L1 phonetic interference during continuous speech.
2. **Low-Resource Languages (e.g., Taiwanese Hokkien / Tâi-gî):** Evaluates real-time tone sandhi parsing and proves orthography independence by removing text reliance in a language with non-standardized scripts.
3. **Unwritten / Oral-Only Languages:** Evaluates less-common languages and regional spoken dialects through direct native-speaker audio recording with international peer collaborators, proving that the zero-text foundational sensory framework can rapidly generate functional acquisition modules without pre-existing commercial datasets.

---

## 5. Evaluation Plan
* **Pilot Design:** Comparative testing between Ground Level zero-text modules and text-first control group.
* **Primary Metric:** Auditory processing latency (ms) and two-option tone/phoneme discrimination accuracy under unscripted listening conditions.

# APPENDIX: Level 0 Operational Frameworks

## Appendix A: Level 0 Pronunciation Framework

### 1. Goal
Level 0 pronunciation is the foundational step from which all other steps are built. The main focus of this section is to train the user's ears to accurately perceive the phonemes and tonality of the target language and how they combine in natural, native speech. 

Vocabulary used in this stage is purely illustrative so the user can hear how sounds naturally combine in speech. Any meanings learned in this section are purely incidental.

### 2. Ear Training & Acoustic Discrimination
* **Phoneme Isolation:** Training the ear to distinguish between subtle foreign sound contrasts (e.g., aspirated vs. unaspirated consonants) before L1 phonetic filtering distorts the input.
* **Pitch & Tonality (Humming):** Isolating pitch contours ($F_0$) from speech by stripping vocalization down to raw humming patterns, allowing learners to parse tone direction without lexical distraction. There are no visual representations of pitch (e.g., no "rising tone" text descriptions or directional symbols), as informing adult learners on how a tone "should" sound often leads to strange and incorrect vocalizations.
* **Duration & Rhythm:** Training sensitivity to syllable length, vowel nuclei, and natural speech cadences.

### 3. Descriptive Sound Mapping
* **Descriptive over Prescriptive:** Focuses strictly on how native speakers actually pronounce sounds in continuous, connected speech—including slurring, tone sandhi, and dropped syllables—rather than textbook rules.
* **Zero Script Reliance:** No alphabets, IPA text, or written graphemes (Pinyin, Romaji, Hán-jī) are shown on screen.
* **Category Distinctions:** Abstract visual symbols are used solely to distinguish pitch directions or phoneme groupings without prescribing target sounds through written text.

### 4. Illustrative Vocabulary & Incidental Meaning
* **Sound-First Exposure:** Audio samples are chosen primarily to demonstrate phonetic combinations rather than build a functional lexicon.
* **Incidental Learning:** Semantic comprehension is secondary at this phase. While visual icons may accompany sound samples, the primary goal remains pure auditory perception and pitch contour parsing.

### 5. Progression to Concrete Readers
Once basic acoustic discrimination is established, the learner transitions to the Level 0 Story and Level 0 Vocabulary Frameworks, where these parsed sounds are systematically linked to concrete sensory words and narratives (e.g., animal POVs).

## Appendix B: Level 0 Vocabulary Framework

### 1. Goal
Level 0 vocabulary is designed to build immediate acoustic phoneme recognition and direct visual-sensory associations without using written text, translation, or L1 dictionary definitions. 

By restricting targets to high-frequency, physical, and sensory concepts, Level 0 enables adult learners to form raw mental connections to foreign spoken sounds before any graphemes or alphabets are introduced.

### 2. Core Vocabulary Selection Principles
1. **Direct Visual Representability:** Every target word must be easily representable by an unambiguous picture, GIF, or short vector animation (e.g., *dog, cat, tree, run, eat, sit*).
2. **Zero Abstraction:** Abstract terms (e.g., *time, money, work, opinion, freedom*) are strictly prohibited.
3. **Animal & Physical Context:** Words are chosen based on immediate physical interaction with the environment (physical states, physical objects, locations, spatial movements).
4. **Universal Recognition:** Concepts are universally understood across cultures without requiring cultural background knowledge.

### 3. Semantic Categories & Concrete Word Types

| Category | Example Concepts | Visual Asset Format |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Entities / Nouns** | Cat, dog, box, tree, water, sun, floor, human | Minimalist Vector / Photo |
| **Physical Actions / Verbs** | See, smell, walk, jump, sit, eat, drink, sleep | Short Looping GIF / Animation |
| **Physical States / Adjectives** | Big, small, hungry, sleepy, warm, cold | Color-Coded / Expression Symbol |
| **Spatial / Directional** | In, on, under, up, down | Directional Arrow / Overlay Symbol |

### 4. Non-Textual Lookup & Visual Anchoring
* **Audio-First Trigger:** The learner always hears the authentic native audio clip *before* or *simultaneously* with visual exposure.
* **Symbolic Dictionary:** If a learner needs to "look up" a word during a reader or exercise, the lookup displays a 2-second looping animation or vector illustration—never a text translation.
* **No Graphemes:** Neither target language scripts (e.g., Hán-jī, Pinyin, Romaji) nor native language text (English) appear on screen.

### 5. Modular Expansion & Repetition Cap
* **Target Density:** 30–50 unique concrete vocabulary words per story module.
* **Controlled High Repetition:** Each target word repeats 5–10 times across varying visual contexts within a single narrative arc (e.g., *Cat sees box / Cat jumps in box / Cat sleeps in box*).
* **Incremental Introduction:** Each new narrative in a character series introduces only 3–5 new concrete concepts while reinforcing previously acquired acoustic patterns.

## Appendix C: Level 0 Story Framework & Rules

### 1. Goal
Level 0 stories are audio-only graded readers designed to build foundational semantic understanding through simple narratives and controlled repetition. 

By grounding every story in concrete, sensory reality and removing all reliance on written text, these readers allow adult learners to form direct neural associations between spoken sounds and physical concepts.

### 2. Narrative Constraints
1. **Animal Perspective:** Every narrative is told from the POV of an animal (e.g., cat, dog, bird, snake). Animals naturally interact strictly with physical, sensory reality.
2. **Concrete Primacy:** Focus exclusively on immediate sensory objects, actions, and states (sun, box, floor, walk, jump, hungry, sleepy). Abstract human concepts (time, money, jobs, complex politics) are strictly forbidden.
3. **Mature without Complexity:** Animal POV allows for adult themes (territory, hunting, mating, survival) without introducing complex or abstract vocabulary.

### 3. Linguistic & Structural Rules
* **Zero Text:** Audio output only, paired visually with symbols, pictures, or simple looping animations (GIFs/WebP).
* **Vocabulary Limit:** 30–50 unique high-frequency concrete words per story module.
* **Grammatical Pattern:** Short present-tense sentences (3–6 words maximum per sentence).
* **Controlled Repetition:** Target verbs (look, smell, jump, eat, sleep) and concrete nouns must repeat 5–10 times across varying visual contexts within a single story.

### 4. Character Series & Progression
* **Complete Character Arcs:** Stories are grouped into collections following a single character through a complete narrative arc (Beginning, Middle, End).
* **Modular Continuity:** Each story in a character series introduces 3–5 new concrete vocabulary anchors while reinforcing previously acquired sounds.

### 5. Primer & Review
* **Primers:** Before every story, a collection of the core vocabulary for that story is displayed using visuals paired with an audio button to prime the user's attention to key vocabulary.
* **Review:** At the end of each story, a low-pressure review covers core vocabulary, grammar, and pronunciation in a gamified manner. Users select from simple 2-choice options and receive immediate feedback without points, streaks, or artificial stress, keeping the cognitive load low.
* **Vocabulary Breakdown:** Users have the option to view a descriptive breakdown of individual phonemes and tone contours for each word based on how it is actually pronounced in the story. To keep the focus strictly on ear training, symbols are used solely to distinguish phoneme and tone categories—no symbols indicate exact target sounds.
